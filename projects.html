Peptide inhibitor for the peanut allergen Ara h 2

2025-26 · Delaware Valley Science Fair 2026

Peanut allergy is the leading cause of fatal food-induced anaphylaxis, and most of it is driven by one protein, Ara h 2. I designed a short peptide, RLPYSPSQDP, meant to compete with the sites on Ara h 2 that IgE antibodies bind to, so the immune system doesn't react to the allergen.

I generated and filtered peptide variants for immunogenicity, docked them to the antibody, and ran 20 ns molecular dynamics simulations. The lead peptide stayed stable (backbone RMSD of 0.15 nm) and outperformed the native controls. A second phase simulated a chitosan and PEG matrix to model how the peptide could be delivered through the nose. The project earned a Certificate of Achievement from the Delaware Valley Chapter PDA.

Exploring compression algorithms

2024-25 · Delaware Valley Science Fair 2025

I compared five lossless compression algorithms (Arithmetic coding, Golomb, LZW, Burrows-Wheeler and Huffman) on 245 files across seven file types. I wrote each algorithm in Python and recorded compression ratio, compression time and decompression time.

Text compressed the most, with Arithmetic and Huffman coding cutting it by roughly 56 to 80 percent. Files that are already compressed, like JPEG and MP4, barely changed, and Golomb coding made most files larger. Which algorithm is best depends on the file type and whether speed or size matters more.
